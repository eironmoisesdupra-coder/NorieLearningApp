# Grade 12 Science: first four lessons

Reviewed 2026-10-04. Lesson prose, numerical examples, questions and local schematics are original. The references below informed scientific checks; no source illustrations or remote runtime resources were added. Ecology has its own [source memo](science-grade12-ecology-sources.md). This pack covers Grade 12 only; College remains starter content.

## Genetics & Molecular Biology

- OpenStax Biology 2e, [Prokaryotic Transcription](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/15-2-prokaryotic-transcription): template use and synthesis of RNA in the 5′ to 3′ direction.

The lesson explicitly distinguishes template DNA from coding DNA, transcription from translation, sequence from expression, and molecular association from causal evidence. Supplied codon meanings make the decoding exercises self-contained. The simplified autosomal recessive model states its assumptions rather than generalizing to all traits.

Numerical and sequence audit: template 3′-TAC CTT-5′ gives RNA 5′-AUG GAA-3′ and the supplied methionine–glutamate fragment. Template AAA gives UUU, specifying phenylalanine in the supplied code. GAA and GAG both specify glutamate. Two independent one-half transmission probabilities give one-quarter aa probability per offspring, not an exact four-child ratio.

## Chemical Equilibrium

- OpenStax Chemistry, [Chapter 13 Summary](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry/pages/13-summary): dynamic equilibrium, reaction quotients, equilibrium constants and catalyst effects.
- OpenStax Chemistry, [Shifting Equilibria: Le Châtelier’s Principle](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry/pages/13-3-shifting-equilibria-le-chateliers-principle): concentration, pressure and temperature changes under specified conditions.

The lesson uses the introductory dilute concentration approximation and distinguishes it from dimensionless thermodynamic activities. Gas-volume predictions specify fixed temperature and an ideal mixture. Adding inert gas specifies fixed volume; catalyst effects are separated from temperature changes.

Numerical audit: for A ⇌ B, 0.80/0.20 = 4, reversal gives K = 0.25, and initial A = 1.00 with B = 0 gives equilibrium A = 0.20 and B = 0.80 at K = 4. A total of 0.50 gives A = 0.10 and B = 0.40. Present A = 0.40 and B = 0.80 gives Q = 2, so net forward change follows when K = 4. For A ⇌ 2B, consumption of 0.20 mol/L A produces 0.40 mol/L B at constant volume.

## Electric Fields

- OpenStax University Physics Volume 2, [Electric Field](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/5-4-electric-field): field definition, signed force, point sources and vector superposition.
- OpenStax University Physics Volume 2, [Electric Potential and Potential Difference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/7-2-electric-potential-and-potential-difference): scalar potential, signed potential differences and potential-energy changes.
- OpenStax Physics, [Electric Potential](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/18-4-electric-potential): uniform-field potential differences and work done by the electric field.

Models specify stationary sources, small test charges, vacuum or the air approximation, and negligible plate-edge effects when appropriate. Force direction is distinguished from instantaneous velocity. External work equals potential-energy change only under the stated unchanged-kinetic-energy and other-work conditions.

Numerical audit: k = 9 × 10⁹, Q = 2 × 10⁻⁹ C and r = 0.30 m give E = 200 N/C; doubling distance gives 50 N/C. A +2 microcoulomb charge in +300 N/C has force +0.0006 N. Contributions +600 and −200 N/C acting on −2 microcoulombs give −0.0008 N. Moving 0.02 m along 200 N/C gives ΔV = −4 V; a +3 microcoulomb charge has ΔU = −12 microjoules and field work +12 microjoules. A −2 microcoulomb charge going from 12 V to 7 V gains 10 microjoules of potential energy. The midpoint picture shows two equal rightward contributions between opposite source charges.

## Geologic Processes

- USGS, [The Laws of Superposition and Cross-Cutting Relations](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/fossils/laws.html): relative ordering of undisturbed strata and younger cutting features.
- USGS, [Using Radioactive Decay to Determine Geologic Age](https://www.usgs.gov/educational-resources/geologic-age-using-radioactive-decay-determine-geologic-age): half-lives and radiometric time.
- USGS, [Age of the Earth](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/geotime/age.html): what radiometric ages record and the consequences of later disturbance.
- USGS, [From Probabilities to Hazard](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/old.2003/fs039-03/hazard.html): probabilistic shaking hazard, exposure and structural vulnerability.

The lesson conditions superposition on an upright, undeformed sequence. It distinguishes a mineral formation or closure age from later sediment deposition, and long-term hazard probabilities from exact event dates. The radioactive-clock picture is an ideal closed system with no initial daughter material; practice half-lives are invented, not assigned to named isotopes.

Numerical audit: two half-lives leave 25% parent; a 100-million-year half-life then gives 200 million years. One eighth remaining with a 50-million-year half-life gives 150 million years. Offset of 30 m in 10,000 years averages 3 mm/year. A 12 cm uncompacted layer accumulated in 600 years averages 0.20 mm/year. Neither rate implies uniform annual motion or deposition.
