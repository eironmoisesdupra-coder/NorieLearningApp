# Grade 10 Ecosystems: authoring and scientific checks

This lesson extends earlier food-web and ecosystem work with population accounting, changing carrying capacity, quantitative production transfers, competing explanations and conservation comparisons. All lesson text, questions and drawings are original. Sources support authoring; no lesson or picture requires network access.

## Sources checked 2026-10-04

- [National Park Service: Cycles and Processes](https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/nature/cyclesprocesses.htm): ecological context, resource limits and interacting processes. A count plateau is not proof of a fixed carrying capacity. The lesson uses invented rabbit counts, not Yellowstone monitoring measurements.
- [National Park Service: The Big Scientific Debate—Trophic Cascades](https://www.nps.gov/articles/the-big-scientific-debate-trophic-cascades.htm): evidence for cascades requires attention to environmental conditions and alternative mechanisms. The lesson avoids claiming that predator return alone necessarily restores vegetation; its predator–herbivore–plant diagram is explicitly conditional.
- [NOAA: Aquatic food webs](https://www.noaa.gov/education/resource-collections/marine-life/aquatic-food-webs): feeding positions and energy transfer through connected food webs.
- [NOAA repository: Energy Flow Through Marine Ecosystems—Confronting Transfer Efficiency](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/57926), Eddy et al., 2021, DOI 10.1016/j.tree.2020.09.006: transfer efficiency varies among ecosystems and processes. The lesson calculates from supplied production rates rather than applying a universal ten-percent rule.
- [NOAA GFDL: Global-scale carbon and energy flows through the planktonic food web](https://www.gfdl.noaa.gov/research_highlight/global-scale-carbon-and-energy-flows-through-the-planktonic-food-web-an-analysis-with-a-coupled-physical-biological-model/): production and trophic transfer are rate-based quantities requiring consistent budgets. Standing biomass is distinguished from energy production.

## Quantitative and instructional checks

- Population balance: 80 + 12 + 3 − 10 − 5 = 80. Independent mastery: 60 + 9 + 4 − 7 − 2 = 64.
- Survey counts 20, 40, 65, 80, 80 yield changes +20, +25, +15, 0. Same area, interval and method specified; resources, disease, predation, detection and movement remain alternative explanations.
- Annual production 10,000 → 1,500 → 150 kJ/m²/year yields 15%, 10%, and 1.5% across both links. Second example 8,000 → 1,600 → 240 yields 20%, 15%, and 3%. The reveal 6,000 → 900 gives 15%; mastery 5,000 → 1,000 gives 20%.
- g/m² is a standing mass per area; kJ/m²/year is annual energy production per area. No conversion between them is assumed.
- Restoration example changes 30% → 50% versus 30% → 35% give +20 and +5 percentage points, and a 15-point difference in changes. The result is suggestive, not proof of causation from two unreplicated sites.
- Twenty practice items follow the shared 7 foundation / 7 intermediate / 6 application grouping; three independent mastery items follow. All assessed mechanisms and methods appear in the lesson. No field collection, chemicals or network are needed for the paper activity.

## Original picture contracts

`g10-population`: five horizontal bars on a shared zero baseline, full width 100 rabbits. Values and year labels render as natural-height Flutter text. These invented counts are not a fitted logistic model.

`g10-energy`: bars proportional to 10,000, 1,500 and 150, sharing a zero baseline. The 1.5%-width final bar is deliberately small. Common units, denominators and detrital/heat pathways are explained in visible text and semantics.

`g10-cascade`: vertically connected labeled states show possible causal effects, with explanatory text beside each step. Arrows do not claim an energy magnitude or a guaranteed ecosystem response.

The grade's additional original pictures show resistance proportions in different generations, neutral Na/Mg/Cl shell counts, a pH concentration ratio, and an ideal resistor circuit.
