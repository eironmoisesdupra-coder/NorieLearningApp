# Grade 2 Science authoring and factual review

The five lessons and 115 assessment items are authored locally. Their explanatory prose and figures are original. Core content has no network dependency. Source links below support author-time factual checks; they are not required to read the lessons.

- **Life Cycles:** [Florida Museum: Butterfly Life Cycle](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/educators/resource/butterfly-life-cycle/) checked egg, caterpillar/larva, pupa/chrysalis and adult stages. The cycle explicitly distinguishes development within one animal from adult reproduction and the next generation. Bean examples cover root/shoot growth, seedlings, flowers, pods and new seeds without claiming all seeds require light to germinate.
- **Habitats:** [Smithsonian Gardens: Creating Bird Habitat at Home](https://gardens.si.edu/gardens/urban-bird-habitat/creating-bird-habitat-at-home/) checked food, water and shelter. [U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service habitat education booklet](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-I49-PURL-gpo93434/pdf/GOVPUB-I49-PURL-gpo93434.pdf) checked space as an animal habitat need. Examples distinguish a nest from the wider habitat and freshwater from salty ocean conditions. Deserts are defined by scarce rainfall, not by universal heat.
- **States of Matter:** [American Chemical Society: Grade 2 Lesson 5.1 background](https://www.acs.org/content/dam/acsorg/education/k-8/inquiry-in-action/second-grade/g2-l5.1-bkgd.pdf) checked melting/freezing and particle motion. [ACS: Matter—Solids, Liquids, and Gases](https://www.acs.org/middleschoolchemistry/lessonplans/chapter1.html) checked the particle model. Prose distinguishes hardness from state, individual solid rice grains from a pourable pile, changed container shape from changed amount, and invisible vapor from visible liquid mist. The vapor figure refers only to mist; clouds can include ice crystals and are not used as a liquid-only example.
- **Light & Sound:** [NASA: Noise—Good Vibrations](https://www.nasa.gov/stem-content/noise-good-vibrations/) checked pitch and rate of vibration. Light examples distinguish sources from reflection and describe the shadow as a less-lit region. Shadow enlargement is limited to an opaque card moving toward a torch while torch and wall remain fixed. Loudness is kept distinct from pitch.
- **Earth & Sky:** [NASA: Why is there day and night?](https://starchild.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/StarChild/questions/question31.html) and [NASA: What Is Earth?](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-is-earth-grades-5-8/) checked rotation and roughly 24-hour daily turning. [NASA: Moonlight](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moonlight/) checked reflected sunlight. [NASA: Why Can You See the Moon During the Day?](https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/why-can-you-see-the-moon-during-the-day-we-asked-a-nasa-scientist-episode-19/) checked daytime Moon observations. The morning/day/evening/night cycle is explicitly a usual local pattern rather than a claim that every latitude has sunrise every day.

Review notes: exactly 20 practice items plus 3 independent mastery items per lesson, with four options and one intended correct answer. Alternatives were revised to test plausible taught misconceptions rather than unrelated or joke answers. Activities use observation, adult help, a battery torch, room-temperature materials and quiet sounds. No tasting, hot apparatus, wildlife relocation or direct solar viewing is requested. First prerequisite is null to preserve the existing grade entry; other prerequisites link the five established Grade 2 lesson IDs in order.
