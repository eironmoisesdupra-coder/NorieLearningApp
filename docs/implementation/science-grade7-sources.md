# Grade 7 Science: authoring and factual checks

Authored 2026-10-03 for the existing five-topic Grade 7 path. Sources were checked
during authoring. They support maintainer review and are not runtime dependencies:
all lesson text, assessments, figure metadata and programmatic pictures remain
available locally. Explanations, numerical datasets and diagrams are original;
no external artwork or source passages were copied into this pack.

## Scientific Investigation

- [Science Buddies: variables](https://www.sciencebuddies.org/science-fair-projects/science-fair/variables)
  checks independent, dependent and controlled variables and isolating a change
  in a beginner controlled experiment.
- [Science Buddies: experimental procedures](https://www.sciencebuddies.org/science-fair-projects/science-fair/writing-experimental-procedures)
  supports a repeatable method with equipment, quantities and controlled conditions.
- [Science Buddies: increasing ability to measure an effect](https://www.sciencebuddies.org/science-fair-projects/competitions/experimental-design-increasing-signal-to-noise)
  checks repeated measurements, trial order and reducing the effect of variation.

The example uses one cart across trials, two surfaces, matched ramp height/slope,
no-push release and a marked 0.60 m timed route. Paired pictures compare conditions,
not unverified identical-looking carts. The data are hypothetical:
A = 1.8, 2.0, 2.2 s; B = 2.8, 3.0, 3.2 s. Independently calculated means are
2.0 and 3.0 s and both ranges are 0.4 s. The third set, 2.1, 2.4, 2.7 s,
has mean 2.4 s and range 0.6 s. Three readings totaling 12.6 s have mean 4.2 s;
4.4 − 4.0 = 0.4 s.

Repeated values are distinguished from accuracy: a shared clock or route error
can persist. Correlation does not establish cause; means cannot repair confounding.
Conclusions are limited to the tested conditions, and unexpected trials are not
deleted just because they conflict with predictions. Practical work uses a
teacher-supervised small cart on a low stable surface with a stopping barrier,
or the local dataset alone.

## Cells & Microscopy

- [OpenStax, Microbiology: properties of light](https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/2-1-the-properties-of-light)
  checks the distinction among magnification, resolution and contrast.
- [OpenStax, Microbiology: instruments of microscopy](https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/2-3-instruments-of-microscopy)
  checks the transmitted-light compound-microscope arrangement and role of lenses.
- [OpenStax, College Physics 2e: microscopes](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/26-4-microscopes)
  supports combined lens magnification and restricted specimen field of view.
- [OpenStax, Biology for AP Courses: studying cells](https://openstax.org/books/biology-ap-courses/pages/4-1-studying-cells)
  checks resolving power, image limitations and the distinction between larger
  images and distinguishable details.

Lens calculations are 10 × 4 = 40×; 10 × 10 = 100×; 10 × 40 = 400×;
10 × 20 = 200×. The 4.0 mm field at 100× and 1.0 mm at 400× are supplied example
calibrations for one eyepiece setup, not universal instrument specifications.
Independently checked size estimates: 1.2 mm / 6 = 0.2 mm = 200 μm;
0.9 mm / 3 = 0.3 mm = 300 μm; 1.0 mm / 4 = 0.25 mm = 250 μm.

The lesson distinguishes a specimen field diameter from the physical eyepiece
opening. Scale bars remain proportional when resized with an image; printed
magnification may not. Stains can improve contrast without revealing natural
colors or species. The question about a stained nucleus explicitly states the
observed contrast improvement. Unresolved structures are not assumed absent,
and a textbook model is not treated as a classroom micrograph. Use prepared slides
or provided images, with no body-fluid collection, tissue cutting or microbial culture.

## Matter & Particles

- [OpenStax, Chemistry 2e: kinetic-molecular theory](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/9-5-the-kinetic-molecular-theory)
  checks random gas-particle motion, wall collisions, pressure and thermal effects.
- [OpenStax, Chemistry 2e: Chapter 9 summary](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/9-summary)
  supports diffusion as net spreading associated with concentration differences.
- [OpenStax, Chemistry 2e: phases and classification of matter](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/1-2-phases-and-classification-of-matter)
  checks physical states and conservation of material during physical changes.
- [OpenStax, College Physics 2e: fluids](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/11-1-what-is-a-fluid)
  checks state properties and liquids/gases as fluids.
- [OpenStax, College Physics: phase changes](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics/pages/13-5-phase-changes)
  checks heat transfer during a state change without an obligatory immediate
  temperature increase under the stated pure-substance/fixed-pressure conditions.

Solid particles vibrate; the ordered-dot panel represents a crystalline example
rather than every solid. Gas compression/expansion changes gaps, not molecule
sizes. The gas-pressure comparison fixes amount and temperature; warming is
separately compared in rigid closed and flexible approximately constant-pressure
containers. These are models, not heating instructions. Temperature concerns
average motion energy and is distinguished from total thermal energy.

The diffusion picture retains six blue and six amber particles in both panels.
Random motion permits individual movement against the net direction and continues
after mixing. The food-color observation does not claim to isolate diffusion from
bulk currents. Closed melting data preserve 80 + 60 = 140 g; the open-container
example leaves 136 − 80 = 56 g water and transfers 140 − 136 = 4 g outside.
No water enters/leaves the closed example, its outside is dry, and heat can cross
the material boundary. The only learner observation uses room-temperature water
and known food coloring without tasting or heating.

## Force & Motion

- [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1: forces](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/5-1-forces)
  checks net force as a directed sum of forces acting on the selected object.
- [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1: Newton’s first law](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/5-2-newtons-first-law)
  checks balanced forces preserving rest or constant velocity, rather than
  requiring an object to stop.
- [OpenStax, Physics: acceleration](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/3-1-acceleration)
  checks acceleration as the rate of change of velocity, including direction.
- [OpenStax, College Physics 2e: graphical analysis](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/2-8-graphical-analysis-of-one-dimensional-motion)
  supports interpreting slopes from changes in plotted distance/position and time.

The original picture deliberately plots **cumulative traveled distance**, not
signed position: points (0 s, 0 m), (2 s, 4 m), (4 s, 4 m), (6 s, 8 m).
The interval speeds are 2, 0 and 2 m/s. Overall 8 / 6 ≈ 1.33 m/s includes the
pause; the graph does not reveal direction, road shape or numerical force.
Cumulative distance cannot decrease on a return trip, unlike a position graph.

Other independent calculations: 12 / 6 = 2 m/s; 12 / 4 = 3 m/s;
600 cm = 6 m and 6 / 3 = 2 m/s; a 4 m out-and-back route totals 8 m and gives
8 / 4 = 2 m/s. Opposing resultants 8 − 3, 9 − 4 and 7 − 2 are each 5 N right
in their respective scenarios. A leftward-moving cart with rightward net force
initially slows; force direction is not assumed to equal motion direction.
Interaction pairs on different objects are not incorrectly canceled on one object.
Acceleration is explicitly a **rate**, not simply a velocity difference. The
out-and-back distractor set differs from the stationary-interval answer set.

## Earth Systems

- [NASA GPM: connect the spheres](https://gpm.nasa.gov/education/node/173)
  checks the four-system model, their interactions and the hydrosphere including
  groundwater, frozen water and atmospheric water.
- [NASA SVS: Earth system science spheres](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/31365)
  supports interconnected systems and cryosphere as a useful further subdivision.
- [USGS Water Science School: water cycle](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/water-cycle)
  checks evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration,
  runoff and water stores as a branching system.
- [NASA Earth Observatory: carbon cycle](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-carbon-cycle/)
  checks photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition and carbon stores across
  biological and geological timescales.
- [USGS: groundwater development and water budgets](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/circ1186/html/gw_dev.html)
  checks a water budget as an accounting of inputs, outputs and change in storage.
- [USGS: water budgets](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/cir1308)
  supports comparing flows and storage with defined regions and additional
  groundwater processes in a real investigation.
- [USDA Climate Hubs: retaining ground cover](https://www.climatehubs.usda.gov/approach/increase-and-retain-ground-cover-prevent-soil-loss)
  checks protection from water and wind erosion.
- [USDA ARS: vegetation and soil erosion](https://www.ars.usda.gov/research/publications/publication/?seqNo115=142826)
  checks susceptibility after reduced cover and how severe topsoil loss can
  limit support for the former vegetation. The feedback example is conditional.

Water budget values are hypothetical and apply only to the stated period and
boundary. Independently checked: initial 40 + rain 100 − runoff 30 − vapor 50 =
60 L final, a 20 L increase; initial 35 + input 80 − runoff 20 − vapor 30 =
65 L final, a 30 L increase. Infiltration that stays inside the plot is not
subtracted again. Storage increase is not final storage or another outflow.
Unmeasured flows and uncertain measurements are considered before proposing
violation of material conservation.

Plants respire as well as photosynthesize. Carbon pathways are comparison
branches, avoiding a false compulsory linear route. The landscape distinguishes
water-material arrows from Sun input and outgoing energy; material cycling does
not recycle heat into useful sunlight. Plant-loss/erosion feedback is a conditional
example at a vulnerable site, not a universal prediction. Practical work is a
safe-path observation or local images/data with no river, slope or drainage access.
