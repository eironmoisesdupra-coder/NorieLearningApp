# Grade 6 Science: authoring and factual checks

Authored 2026-10-03 for the existing five-topic Grade 6 sequence. References were
checked during authoring; they are a bibliography for maintainers, not runtime
dependencies. Core explanations, assessments and figure specifications are local.
The text, hypothetical datasets and diagrams are original educational work; no
external photographs, illustrations or source passages were copied into the pack.

## Organisms & Classification

- [OpenStax, Concepts of Biology: introduction to diversity](https://openstax.org/books/concepts-biology/pages/13-introduction)
  supports the introductory Bacteria / Archaea / Eukarya model and the distinction
  between cells without nuclei and eukaryotic cells. The lesson treats this as an
  introductory classification model, rather than a full molecular phylogeny.
- [OpenStax, Biology: characteristics of fungi](https://openstax.org/books/biology/pages/24-1-characteristics-of-fungi)
  verifies nutrient absorption, fungal cell walls, and unicellular yeast as an
  exception to the false claim that every fungus is multicellular.
- [NCBI Bookshelf, Molecular Biology of the Cell: diversity of genomes](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26866/)
  supports molecular evidence for broad groups and explains why appearance alone
  is inadequate for distinguishing Bacteria from Archaea.

The identification key is deliberately limited to pigeon, frog, ant and earthworm.
Backbone presence selects the feather branch; backbone absence selects the
six-jointed-leg branch. Endpoints and every yes/no route were checked independently.
The key figure uses comparison metadata and a branched picture, preventing the
generic process renderer from falsely implying that questions 2 and 3 are
successive steps. Scientific naming distinguishes genus from specific epithet.

## Mixtures & Solutions

- [OpenStax, Chemistry 2e: dissolution process](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/11-1-the-dissolution-process)
  verifies solutions as homogeneous mixtures and particles dispersed through a
  solvent. The lesson distinguishes molecular sugar from salt's dissolved ions
  without requiring Grade 6 learners to calculate ionic quantities.
- [OpenStax, Chemistry: solubility](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry/pages/11-3-solubility)
  verifies a dissolving limit under specified conditions, saturation, and the need
  to qualify temperature effects rather than claim all substances behave alike.
- [OpenStax, Chemistry: Chapter 11 summary](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry/pages/11-summary)
  checks the different typical temperature responses of dissolved gases and many
  dissolved solids.

The 12 g per 100 mL of water solubility limit is explicitly a hypothetical example,
not a published measurement for sugar or salt. Concentration exercises separately
use **final solution volume**: no calculation assumes that final volume equals
starting water volume. Independently checked arithmetic includes
6 / 150 × 100 = 4 g per 100 mL; 8 / 200 × 100 = 4 g per 100 mL;
16 − 12 = 4 g undissolved; 2 × 12 = 24 g capacity in the example;
10 / 200 × 100 = 5 g per 100 mL.

Ordinary filtration retains sand and passes dissolved salt. Evaporation can leave
salt; distillation additionally collects condensed water. Neither a clear sample
nor a separation diagram certifies drinking safety. The only learner activity uses
known sugar and room-temperature water without tasting or heating.

## Electricity

- [U.S. Energy Information Administration: batteries, circuits and transformers](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/batteries-circuits-and-transformers.php)
  checks source, complete circuit and switch relationships.
- [OpenStax, Physics: parallel circuits](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/19-3-parallel-circuits)
  verifies conservation of charge at steady junctions and addition of branch
  currents.
- [OpenStax, College Physics: resistors in series and parallel](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics/pages/21-1-resistors-in-series-and-parallel)
  verifies one-path series connections versus branches across shared junctions.

Voltage (V), current (A) and energy are distinguished. The source supplies energy;
lamps do not consume charges. All numerical readings are supplied model data,
not meter-connection instructions. Independent sums include 0.10 + 0.20 = 0.30 A,
0.15 + 0.25 = 0.40 A, 0.40 − 0.15 = 0.25 A and
0.18 + 0.22 = 0.40 A. A break in one parallel lamp branch is distinguished from
a break in the common supply wire. Practical work is observation of a teacher-approved
low-voltage AA-cell kit or diagrams, with no mains electricity, direct terminal
connections, damaged cells or liquid-conductivity experiments.

## Plate Tectonics

- [USGS: are tectonic plates floating on magma?](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/are-tectonic-plates-floating-magma)
  verifies the crucial distinction between a mostly solid, slowly deforming mantle
  and the inaccurate image of plates floating on a worldwide liquid-magma ocean.
- [USGS: This Dynamic Planet](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pdf/planet.pdf)
  checks divergent, convergent and transform motions and their different crustal
  effects.
- [USGS: seafloor spreading and subduction model](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1999/ofr-99-0132/)
  checks new crust at ridges and rock-age evidence for movement away from them.
- [USGS: eruptions of Hawaiian volcanoes](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/hawaii/page11.html)
  supports within-plate hotspot volcanism as a boundary-pattern exception.

The lesson defines lithosphere as crust plus rigid uppermost mantle; a plate can
contain both land and seafloor. Continental collision is separated from the
oceanic–continental subduction model, and transform movement is not assigned
obligatory volcanism. The 8 / 2 / 2 / 8 million-year ridge dataset and constant
3 cm/year rate are hypothetical instructional data. Calculations independently
checked: 3 × 10 = 30 cm; 3 × 100 = 300 cm = 3 m. Neither average motion nor the
rock-age table supplies the timing or slip of a particular earthquake.

## Solar System

- [IAU: 2006 General Assembly resolution votes](https://www.iau.org/IAU/Iau/News/PR2006/iau-2006-general-assembly-resolution-votes.aspx)
  verifies the Solar System planet criteria, eight planets, dwarf-planet criteria
  including non-satellite status, and Pluto's classification. Clearing refers to
  orbital dominance, not absolutely empty space.
- [NASA: about the planets](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/)
  verifies orbital order and terrestrial, gas-giant and ice-giant groupings.
- [NASA: cosmic distances](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/cosmic-distances/)
  verifies the astronomical unit and its usefulness for Solar System distances.
- [NASA: Sun facts](https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts/)
  verifies nuclear fusion as the Sun's energy source.
- [NASA: Solar System facts](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/solar-system-facts/)
  checks the wider system of moons and smaller bodies, not merely eight planets.

AU is rounded to about 150 million km. The average Sun distances are rounded to
Earth 1.0 AU, Mars 1.5 AU, Jupiter 5.2 AU and Neptune 30.1 AU. The text explicitly
separates Sun distances from changing Earth–planet separations. Independent model
calculations: at 10 cm/AU, Earth 10 cm, Mars 15 cm, Jupiter 52 cm and Neptune
301 cm = 3.01 m; at 5 cm/AU, Earth 5 cm and Jupiter 26 cm. A natural Earth-orbiting
body is a natural satellite; the question specifies natural to exclude artificial
satellites. The orbit-order picture is not a size/distance scale or a dated
planetary configuration.

## Pack review and integration

Each lesson was authored and checked before proceeding to the next. The independent
content reviewer examined all 115 questions and their taught concepts, keys,
explanations and numerical calculations. Minor refinements from review were applied:
the branching-key rendering contract, natural-satellite wording and electrical
distractors that remain within the same domain. The scientific-name item tests
complete two-part order using case-insensitively distinct options, rather than
capitalization contrasts that the quiz helper intentionally normalizes.

The pack contains 5 lessons, 100 practice items (7 foundation, 7 intermediate,
6 application per lesson), 15 independent mastery items and 15 figure
specifications. Picture contracts are `g6-key`, `g6-circuit`, `g6-branches`,
`g6-plates` and `g6-solar`. All titles and prerequisite IDs retain the established
Grade 6 sequence. Authoring checks cover seven-field rows, four distinct options,
unique stems and comparison with prior-grade source text. `dart format` verifies
parsing. Flutter analysis, curriculum tests, widget layout/semantics checks,
actual output inspection and builds belong to the integration pass; this document
does not claim those checks have passed before their results exist.

An author-side final scan found 115 unique stems, no exact stem matches in the
Grade 1–5 source, no repeated complete answer sets, and four distinct normalized
options in every row. Instructional body word counts, excluding objectives,
headings, captions and the key-concept box, are 1,139 (classification),
1,124 (mixtures), 1,109 (electricity), 1,066 (tectonics) and 1,142 (Solar System).
