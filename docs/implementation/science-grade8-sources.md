## Genetics Basics

- [NHGRI: allele](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Allele) checks alleles
  as versions at a gene location.
- [NHGRI: dominant traits and alleles](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Dominant-Traits-and-Alleles)
  checks dominant expression in a heterozygote, distinct from frequency or value.
- [OpenStax Biology 2e: Mendel and probability](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/12-1-mendels-experiments-and-the-laws-of-probability)
  checks probability reasoning and experimental crosses.
- [OpenStax Biology 2e: inheritance laws](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/12-3-laws-of-inheritance)
  checks segregation, Punnett squares and model assumptions.
- [OpenStax Concepts of Biology: inheritance](https://openstax.org/books/concepts-biology/pages/8-2-laws-of-inheritance)
  checks genotype/phenotype, homozygous/heterozygous and inheritance patterns.

The selected diploid plant uses a stated one-gene, two-allele, complete-dominance
model: A gives purple flowers in AA or Aa; aa gives white. This is not a claim
that all flower colors or human traits follow that model. Typical gamete
formation gives one allele per gamete. The model assumes equal segregation,
random fertilization and comparable survival of offspring genotypes.

Aa × Aa yields probabilities 25% AA, 50% Aa and 25% aa; purple probability is
75%. Aa × aa yields 50% Aa and 50% aa. The expected white counts are 12 × 0.25
= 3 and 20 × 0.50 = 10, not guaranteed sample results. Cross outcomes are
independent under the stated assumptions. A white offspring in the test cross
distinguishes an Aa purple parent; a small all-purple sample does not prove AA.
Gamete draws in the paper activity are replaced between trials.

The text distinguishes dominance from commonness, strength and desirability;
mutations are not purposeful responses to need. Polygenic and environmental
influences are introduced without assigning simplistic Mendelian labels to
human height or appearance. Genotype options include descriptive words so AA,
Aa and aa do not become duplicate options under case-insensitive validation.

## Chemical Reactions

- [American Chemical Society: what is a chemical reaction?](https://www.acs.org/middleschoolchemistry/lessonplans/chapter6/lesson1.html)
  checks reactants, products and rearrangement of atoms.
- [OpenStax Chemistry 2e: writing and balancing equations](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/4-1-writing-and-balancing-chemical-equations)
  checks formulas, coefficients, subscripts and atom conservation.
- [ACS: core chemistry concepts](https://www.acs.org/education/policies/middle-and-high-school-chemistry/core-concepts.html)
  checks conservation and energy changes in chemical processes.

The balanced model 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O retains four hydrogen and two oxygen atoms;
two water molecules are not two grams of water. Changing H₂O to H₂O₂ changes
the substance rather than balancing the equation. The guided methane equation
CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O has one carbon, four hydrogen and four oxygen atoms on
each side. Ordinary chemical reactions rearrange atoms, not nuclei.

Mass examples: contents 10 g + 15 g = 25 g; adding a 35 g container gives a
60 g total before and after a closed-system reaction. A 10 g metal gaining
3 g oxygen has 13 g product. An open measured system changing from 60 g to
57 g has 3 g outside the chosen boundary if that is the only transfer.
Atom conservation does not require an unchanged number of molecules.

Bubbles, color change, temperature change and a precipitate are qualified
evidence to investigate, not automatic proof: boiling, released dissolved gas,
added dye, external heating and existing sediment can provide alternatives.
Bond breaking requires energy; bond formation releases energy. Net transfer
determines exothermic/endothermic behavior, and initial energy input can still
be needed. The activity uses paper atom models only. It does not instruct
learners to combine cleaners, handle reactive gases, heat chemicals, use flames
or conduct a reaction in a pressurized sealed container.

## Work & Energy

- [OpenStax Physics: work, power and the work–energy theorem](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/9-1-work-power-and-the-work-energy-theorem)
  checks work, joules, kinetic energy and average power.
- [OpenStax College Physics: gravitational potential energy](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics/pages/7-3-gravitational-potential-energy)
  checks near-surface mgh and height-reference dependence.
- [OpenStax University Physics: work](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/7-1-work)
  checks the force/displacement direction and negative or zero work.
- [OpenStax University Physics: work–energy theorem](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/7-3-work-energy-theorem)
  checks net work as the change in kinetic energy.

W = Fd is restricted to a constant force parallel and in the same direction as
the displacement. Opposing force does negative work; perpendicular force does
zero work on the chosen object. Effort in the learner's body is distinguished
from work on an unmoved wall or from work by an upward force on a horizontally
carried object. A joule is a newton-meter; a watt is a joule per second.

The ideal steady lift has 1 kg mass, 1 m upward displacement and model
g = 10 N/kg. Applied force and weight are each 10 N. Applied work is +10 J,
weight work is −10 J, and net work is zero, so kinetic energy stays constant.
The Earth–object system gains 10 J gravitational potential energy. Equal forces
do not imply zero work by each force. The diagram shows the steady segment,
not the starting or stopping acceleration.

KE = ½mv² gives 2 J for 1 kg at 2 m/s, 8 J for 1 kg at 4 m/s and 16 J for
2 kg at 4 m/s. The guided 2 kg, 1.5 m lift requires 30 J with the stated
g; 30 J / 3 s = 10 W. A 60 J transfer in 6 s gives 10 W; in 3 s it gives
20 W. Mechanical energy can transfer to thermal stores and surroundings while
the broader energy account remains conserved. Activity options use a picture
or a small object raised briefly under supervision, without drops or heavy loads.

## Waves

- [OpenStax Physics: wave speed, amplitude, frequency and period](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/13-2-wave-properties-speed-amplitude-frequency-and-period)
  checks spatial and temporal definitions, v = fλ and T = 1/f.
- [OpenStax University Physics: traveling waves](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/16-1-traveling-waves)
  checks mechanical media, transverse/longitudinal models and energy propagation.
- [OpenStax University Physics: mathematics of waves](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/16-2-mathematics-of-waves)
  checks distinguishing a spatial profile from a material particle's trajectory.

The original spatial picture has amplitude 0.5 m and neighboring crests 2 m
apart. Crest-to-trough height is 1 m. Frequency 3 Hz is supplied separately,
not inferred from the snapshot; v = 3 × 2 = 6 m/s and T = 1/3 s. Five cycles
in 2 s give 2.5 Hz; 4 Hz gives 0.25 s. At 12 m/s and 4 Hz, λ = 3 m; at
the same speed and 8 Hz, λ = 1.5 m. A 3 Hz wave entering a medium with
speed 3 m/s has wavelength 1 m.

Sound in air is longitudinal and requires a material medium. Light can cross
a vacuum. Surface-water motion is qualified rather than reduced to a purely
transverse rope model. Wave travel is distinguished from local oscillation,
and the model does not assert that real waves can never transport matter.
Sound pressure graphs do not prove transverse particle motion. At a stationary
boundary with a steady source, frequency stays tied to the source while speed
and wavelength may change. Reflection, transmission and absorption can occur
together; absorbed energy is included in other stores. Safe activity uses
paper or a teacher-supervised soft rope with small movements and a clear area.

## Weather & Climate

- [NOAA: understanding climate normals](https://www.noaa.gov/explainers/understanding-climate-normals)
  checks 30-year references, weather comparisons, quality control and handling
  changed or incomplete station records.
- [NOAA Ocean Today: educator guide](https://oceantoday.noaa.gov/teekandtom/educators-guide/educators-guide.pdf)
  checks latitude, elevation, nearby water, mountains and circulation as regional
  climate influences.
- [NASA: Earth's radiation budget](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/13_radiationbudget/)
  checks incoming shortwave sunlight, reflection, surface absorption and outgoing
  longwave infrared, including direct escape and atmospheric emission.
- [NASA: climate and Earth's energy budget](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/climate-and-earths-energy-budget/)
  checks the greenhouse energy balance and increasing emission as temperature rises.
- [NASA: causes of climate change](https://science.nasa.gov/climate-change/causes/)
  checks human greenhouse gas influences, natural influences and the evidence that
  solar changes do not explain recent global warming.
- [IPCC AR6: adaptation and mitigation glossary](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/annex-ii/)
  checks adjustment to impacts versus reducing greenhouse gas emissions or
  enhancing removals.

All numerical station examples are invented, explicitly labeled teaching data.
Five daily means 24, 26, 25, 27 and 28 °C total 130 and average 26 °C;
their range is 4 °C. They are weather data, not a climate normal. A supplied
30-year annual reference of 25.0 °C compared with a 26.2 °C annual mean at
the same station gives +1.2 °C anomaly. Another 24.3 °C year gives −0.7 °C.
An annual 900 mm precipitation total compared with a 1,000 mm annual reference
gives −100 mm. References must match variable, location and interval.

An anomaly is not an absolute temperature or, on its own, a trend. One station-year
does not establish a global trend. Actual climate normals require more than our
simple mean arithmetic; climate includes variability and seasonal patterns.
The greenhouse picture distinguishes reflected sunlight from infrared absorption
and emission upward/downward, and retains energy routes to space. It does not
show energy held forever or confuse ozone depletion with the greenhouse effect.
Flood drainage illustrates adaptation; lower-emission electricity illustrates
mitigation, with combined purposes allowed. The activity uses safe existing
records rather than exposure to storms, floodwater or dangerous heat.
