# Grade 11 Science: first four lessons

Reviewed 2026-10-04. Lessons, calculations, questions and diagram descriptions are original instructional prose. Sources are fact-check references, not copied lesson text. No external resource is required at runtime. Fifth-lesson references are recorded by its author separately.

## Cell Biology

- OpenStax Biology 2e, [Passive Transport](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/5-2-passive-transport): selective permeability, facilitated diffusion and conditional osmosis predictions.
- OpenStax Biology 2e, [Active Transport](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/5-3-active-transport): electrochemical gradients and direct versus indirect energy coupling.
- OpenStax Biology 2e, [Regulation of Cellular Respiration](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/7-7-regulation-of-cellular-respiration): ATP production and cellular energy demand.
- OpenStax Biology 2e, [Control of the Cell Cycle](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/10-3-control-of-the-cell-cycle): G1, G2 and mitotic checkpoints; qualified connection to uncontrolled proliferation.

Progression from Grades 7/9: goes beyond organelle naming and basic diffusion into permeability assumptions, electrochemical driving forces, energetic coupling, ATP synthase, stage-specific checkpoints and chromosome/chromatid accounting. All osmosis items specify nonpenetrating solutes; DNA counts use the pre-separation centromere convention.

## Stoichiometry

- OpenStax Chemistry 2e, [Reaction Yields](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/4-4-reaction-yields): limiting reactants, theoretical versus actual yield and percent yield.

Progression from reaction balancing and acid/base amount comparisons: entity counts, dimensional mass-to-mole chains, independent product capacities, excess inventory, recovery and anomalous yields. All examples supply rounded atomic masses H1/O16/C12/N14/Ca40 g/mol and use a rounded Avogadro value 6.02 × 10²³ for calculations; the exact mole definition is identified separately.

Numerical audit: 6 g H2 → 3 mol → 54 g water with excess oxygen; 3 mol H2 + 2 mol O2 → 3 mol water + 0.5 mol O2 excess; 8 g H2 + 32 g O2 → 36 g water + 4 g H2 excess; 27/36 = 75%; 25 g CaCO3 → 11 g CO2, 8.8/11 = 80%; mastery 50 g CaCO3 → 22 g CO2, 17.6/22 = 80%.

## Mechanics

- OpenStax University Physics Volume 1, [Work-Energy Theorem](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/7-3-work-energy-theorem): net work and kinetic-energy change.
- OpenStax [University Physics Volume 1](https://assets.openstax.org/oscms-prodcms/media/documents/UniversityPhysicsVol1-WEB.pdf): vector components, constant-acceleration conditions, Newtonian dynamics and system energy accounting.

Progression from Grade 9 force/motion and Grade 8 work/energy: signed one-dimensional kinematics, perpendicular displacement, velocity-time area, third-law system distinction and cross-checking force/kinematics with work-energy. Assumptions explicitly include constant mass, inertial frame, constant acceleration, particle-like object and system boundary where needed. Gravitational numerical examples supply g = 10 m/s².

Numerical audit: 6 m east/2 m west in4s → distance8m, displacement4m, speed2m/s, velocity1m/s east; 3/4 perpendicular components →5m; 10−4N on3kg →2m/s²; u2,a2,t3 →v8,s15; ΔK = 96−6 =90J =6N×15m; u6,a−2,t3 →v0,s9; 2kg×10m/s²×3m =60J.

## Earth Materials

- USGS [Mohs Hardness Scale](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/mohs-hardness-scale): reference mineral ordering.
- USGS [Natural Gemstones: Mineral Gemstones](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/gemstones/mineral.html): quartz hardness7 and feldspar range6–6.5. The lesson uses the supplied introductory feldspar reference6, not a universal exact value for every feldspar.
- USGS [What are igneous rocks?](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-are-igneous-rocks): crystallization and cooling-rate texture interpretation.
- USGS [What are sedimentary rocks?](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-are-sedimentary-rocks): clastic formation, burial, compaction and cementation.
- USGS [What are metamorphic rocks?](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-are-metamorphic-rocks): precursor rocks and heat/pressure/fluid-driven changes.
- USGS [Collecting Rocks](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/collect1/collectgip.html): rock groups, variable cooling/composition and context recording.
- USGS [Weeks 9–12 educational resources](https://www.usgs.gov/educational-resources/whats-new-weeks-9-12): mineral properties and multiple rock-cycle pathways.

Progression from Grade4 rock groups and Grades6/9 plate processes: diagnostic property combinations, ordinal hardness, fracture versus cleavage, candidate-set limitations, sorting, two-stage cooling, foliation and preserved multi-stage histories. No exact age, depth or environment is inferred from one texture. Physical work is optional; supplied observations and photographs allow fully offline, dust-free study.
