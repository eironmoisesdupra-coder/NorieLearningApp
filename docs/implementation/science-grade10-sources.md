# Grade 10 Science: sources and authoring checks

The five lessons preserve the existing titles, order and stable prerequisites. All prose, assessment items and diagrams are original; the sources below support scientific verification, and no lesson requires network access. Sources checked 2026-10-04. Ecosystems is independently authored in `grade_10_ecosystems.dart`; its references and quantitative review are recorded in [science-grade10-ecosystems-sources.md](science-grade10-ecosystems-sources.md).

## Evolution

- [UC Museum of Paleontology, Understanding Evolution: Natural selection](https://evolution.berkeley.edu/evolution-101/mechanisms-the-processes-of-evolution/natural-selection/): variation, heredity and differential reproduction; selection acts on available variation, without foresight.
- [NHGRI: Genetic drift](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Genetic-Drift): random allele-frequency changes and particularly strong effects in small populations.
- [UC Museum of Paleontology: Lines of evidence](https://evolution.berkeley.edu/lines-of-evidence/): converging fossil, homology and other evidence for common ancestry.
- [OpenStax Biology 2e, Population evolution](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/19-1-population-evolution): population-level allele frequencies, diploid denominators, and the distinction between phenotype and genotype frequencies.

Checks: resistance samples are invented, 10/100 = 10% and 40/100 = 40%; change is 30 percentage points, with fourfold later frequency. Fifty diploid individuals supply 100 copies of one autosomal gene; 30 copies yield 0.30. A count rise from 20/100 to 30/200 is a frequency fall from 20% to 15%. The lesson explicitly separates selection from drift and gene flow; count change alone cannot identify the mechanism. Paper-token activities model mechanisms rather than establish claims about a real species. No microbial culture or treatment activity is proposed.

## Periodic Table

- [Royal Society of Chemistry: Periodic table](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/): element identities, group/period placements, atomic numbers and electron configurations.
- [OpenStax Chemistry 2e, The periodic table](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/2-5-the-periodic-table): modern organization, groups and periods, and main-group classification.
- [OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Periodic variations in element properties](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/6-5-periodic-variations-in-element-properties): qualified radius and first-ionization-energy patterns, nuclear attraction, shielding and configuration-dependent exceptions.

Checks: neutral Na/Mg/Cl counts 2,8,1 / 2,8,2 / 2,8,7; proton counts 11/12/17. Na+, Mg2+, Cl− have 10/10/18 electrons without changing proton count. MgCl2 balances +2 with two −1 charges. Helium's filled first shell has two electrons. The simple valence rules are limited to named main-group examples, not transition metals. Radius/ionization comparisons specify neutral atoms and general trends; neighboring exceptions are acknowledged.

## Acids & Bases

- [OpenStax Chemistry 2e, pH and pOH](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-2-ph-and-poh): logarithmic pH, the dilute concentration approximation, and neutral-water temperature dependence.
- [OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Relative strengths of acids and bases](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-3-relative-strengths-of-acids-and-bases): ionization extent and strong/weak acid-base behavior.
- [OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Classifying chemical reactions](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/4-2-classifying-chemical-reactions): acid-base reactions, spectator ions and net ionic equations.

Checks: pH differences of 1/2/3 correspond to hydronium ratios 10/100/1000, with lower pH higher in hydronium. A tenfold dilution of the supplied dilute strong-acid example raises pH approximately one unit; the near-neutral and weak-acid limitations are explicit. Neutral means equal hydronium/hydroxide concentrations, with pH about 7 specified at 25 °C. Strength and concentration are separate. Neutralization uses one-to-one hydronium/hydroxide bookkeeping; 6−4 = 2 acid units and 7−3 = 4 base units. Equal volumes alone are insufficient. Paper data/tokens replace chemical handling; no tasting or household-cleaner mixing is instructed.

## Electricity & Magnetism

- [OpenStax College Physics 2e, Ohm's law](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/20-2-ohms-law-resistance-and-simple-circuits): current, potential difference, resistance and the ohmic model.
- [OpenStax College Physics 2e, Electric power and energy](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/20-4-electric-power-and-energy): P = VI and E = Pt for steady power.
- [OpenStax College Physics 2e, Resistors in series and parallel](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/21-1-resistors-in-series-and-parallel): shared series current, shared parallel voltage and junction conservation.
- [OpenStax College Physics 2e, Induced emf and magnetic flux](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/23-1-induced-emf-and-magnetic-flux): changing magnetic flux and induced voltage.
- [OpenStax College Physics 2e, Magnetic fields produced by currents](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-9-magnetic-fields-produced-by-currents-amperes-law): current direction, coil fields and the effects of current magnitude and turn count.

Checks: 12 V/6 Ω = 2 A, P = 24 W, E over 30 s = 720 J. Series 2 Ω + 4 Ω gives 6 Ω, 2 A, drops 4 V and 8 V. Parallel 6 Ω/12 Ω at 12 V gives 2 A/1 A, total 3 A, equivalent 4 Ω. Opening one branch preserves the intact branch voltage only under the stated ideal-supply model. Mastery 9 V/3 Ω = 3 A and 27 W. Constant resistance is qualified by temperature/ohmic behavior. Reversing current reverses field direction; induction needs changing flux, and generator output requires mechanical input. Activities use diagrams, without mains or heated-wire experiments.

## Pack structure and visuals

Each lesson has 20 practice items ordered as 7 foundation, 7 intermediate and 6 application, followed by three independent mastery items. Each seven-field source row gives one correct answer, three distinct distractors, explanatory feedback and a concept. The shared builder rotates correct positions; no lesson defines a new runtime mechanism. First-four lessons supply twelve referenced local figures, including the original `g10-selection`, `g10-periodic`, `g10-ph` and `g10-circuit` pictures; the fifth module contributes three further figures. Figures explicitly label schematic assumptions. No third-party images or textbook paragraphs are embedded.
