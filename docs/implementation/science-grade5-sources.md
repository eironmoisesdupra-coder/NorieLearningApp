# Grade 5 Science authoring evidence

The five lessons were authored individually in the existing order: Cells Introduction; Food Webs; Properties of Matter; Simple Machines; Water Cycle. Each was checked for its taught concepts, answer keys, misconceptions and structural requirements before moving to the next. Every lesson has four measurable objectives, explicit instruction, worked and guided reasoning, answer reveals, a safe observation/activity, common mistakes, quick check, recap, three meaningful figure specifications, twenty practice questions and three independent mastery questions. Difficulty is authored as seven foundation, seven intermediate and six application items, followed by mastery.

## Scientific references

These primary educational references were consulted during authoring on 2026-10-03. Their information is paraphrased in original grade-appropriate prose; no external image is copied.

- [NHGRI: Nucleus](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Nucleus): genetic instructions in a typical plant or animal cell nucleus. The lesson says **most** instructions because other organelles can also contain genetic material.
- [NHGRI: Mitochondria](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Mitochondria): release of usable energy from food and differing energy demands in specialized cells. The lesson includes mitochondria in both plant and animal models and avoids claiming machines or cells create energy.
- [OpenStax Biology 2e: Eukaryotic Cells](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells): membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, wall, chloroplasts and central vacuole. Leaf-cell comparisons are identified as models of photosynthetic cells; chloroplasts are not asserted to occur in all plant cells.
- [BBC Biology: Plant and Animal Cells transcript](https://bam.files.bbci.co.uk/bam/live/content/zqqvh4j/transcript): specialized root hair cell contact and the common absence of chloroplasts in underground root cells. This source supplements the primary cell references for the exception rather than defining the entire lesson.
- [National Park Service: Create Your Own Marine Food Web](https://www.nps.gov/caco/planyourvisit/create-your-own-marine-food-web.htm): connected chains, feeding roles, producers, consumers and decomposers. The original meadow model has selected explicit links and conditional predictions; it is not a claim that every hawk or frog species follows this exact diet.
- [National Park Service: Cycles and Processes](https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/nature/cyclesprocesses.htm): producers, predation, decomposition and material movement. Food-energy arrows point toward eaters; materials returning to soil are distinguished from energy spreading as heat.
- [American Chemical Society: What Is Density?](https://www.acs.org/middleschoolchemistry/lessonplans/chapter3/lesson1.html): mass divided by volume, amount versus density and equal-volume comparisons. All lesson calculations were independently recomputed: 40/20 = 2, 20/20 = 1, 30/10 = 60/20 = 24/8 = 18/6 = 3 g/mL. One mL occupies one cm³.
- [American Chemical Society: Using Dissolving to Identify an Unknown](https://www.acs.org/middleschoolchemistry/lessonplans/chapter5/lesson5.html): controlled dissolving comparisons and solubility under specified conditions. Rate is distinguished from the maximum amount dissolved. Activities use only familiar specified materials and do not invite tasting or unknown-substance tests.
- [NASA: Simple Machines classroom connection](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/simple-machines-classroom-connection-508.pdf): six machine forms and familiar uses.
- [OpenStax Physics: Simple Machines](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/9-3-simple-machines): lever arms, fixed-pulley direction, force/distance trade and friction. The lesson uses conceptual Grade 5 treatment, not secondary-school mechanical-advantage equations. Example force values are not universal ramp predictions: 12 − 6 = 6 N, and 6/12 = 1/2.
- [USGS Water Science School: Water Cycle](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/water-cycle): multiple stores and pathways, including human changes to surfaces, rather than a compulsory circular schedule.
- [USGS: Evaporation and the Water Cycle](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/evaporation-and-water-cycle): liquid-to-vapor change without requiring boiling and its energy supply.
- [USGS: The Atmosphere and the Water Cycle](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/atmosphere-and-water-cycle): invisible vapor, cloud droplets/ice and condensation.
- [USGS: Evapotranspiration and the Water Cycle](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/evapotranspiration-and-water-cycle): plant uptake/transpiration route alongside evaporation.

## Original diagrams and limits

All fifteen figures are local data rendered by shared comparison, process and bar primitives. Four also include dedicated original programmatic pictures in `science_grade5_picture.dart`: `g5-cells`, `g5-food-web`, `g5-lever` and `g5-water-paths`. The models require no remote assets, generated runtime AI or network access. All teaching labels use scalable Flutter text with complete accessible descriptions. Numbered markers help locate geometry but are not the only source of labels.

The paired cell models visibly distinguish the plant wall and inner membrane, include mitochondria in both cells, and identify the plant as photosynthetic. Shape, proportions, numbers and colors are model choices; the models are not microscopy photographs. The branched web has five food-to-eater arrows and two explicit chains. Decomposition and material cycling are taught in a separate figure, preventing a misleading energy-return arrow. The lever puts its pivot closer to the load than to the effort and distinguishes force/travel from mass. Water paths branch into runoff, infiltration and plant uptake/transpiration; groundwater is represented within ground spaces, and the diagram does not imply a fixed route or residence time.

Bar diagrams show observed rabbit counts, equal-volume masses, force-meter readings and collected runoff in clearly labeled units. Example values are original teaching data, not measurements from a real field study. They explicitly limit causal or universal conclusions. Runoff differences were recomputed: 80 − 25 = 55 mL; 100 − 25 = 75 mL uncollected, whose destination remains uncertain.

## Validation handoff

Core explanatory prose, counting the individually written multiline instruction passages and excluding objectives, quizzes and diagram labels: Cells 902 words; Food Webs 887; Properties of Matter 843; Simple Machines 848; Water Cycle 896. Short recap/common-mistake/quick-check passages add instruction beyond these counts.

Static author checks found exactly twenty-three seven-field question rows per lesson, 115 unique stems and four distinct answer choices in each row. The independent reviewer separately checked all 115 keys and explanations, all numerical results, domain coverage and grade progression; requested weak distractors were revised to concrete conceptual or evidence errors. Picture semantics, 244-pixel rendering at 1.3 text scale, and a 260-by-5000 gallery are specified in `test/science_grade5_picture_test.dart`. The coordinating agent runs actual Flutter analysis, tests, builds and visual inspection after integrating the exports and picture whitelist. This handoff does not claim those runtime checks have passed.
