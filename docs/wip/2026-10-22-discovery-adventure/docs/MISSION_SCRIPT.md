# Script and learning design (editorial draft)

**Case:** The Case of the Vanishing Water. **Time:** 5–8 minutes. **Subject:** Science. **Age band:** approximately Grades 4–6, pending teacher review.

## Objectives
1. State that evaporation changes liquid water to water vapor.
2. Differentiate evaporation from spilling/leaking based on observations.
3. Compare water loss in otherwise equivalent warm/cool conditions.
4. Apply this reasoning to a second example and explain using evidence.

## Scenes and states
- Start: Norie notices the water level is lower. "Something changed here. Want to investigate?"
- Clues: dry tabletop (no visible spill); open water container (exposed surface); warmer window (hypothesis prompt, not proof). Prompt: "Which clues could help us test the idea?"
- Experiment: Two equal simulated dishes, one warmer and one cooler, same initial water level, exposure and time. Results qualitatively show greater evaporation in warm trial. "What did we keep the same?"
- Hypothesis: Wrong choices produce gentle feedback: "The evidence doesn't show a spill. What else can happen to liquid water?"
- Independent transfer: "Why can damp clothes dry even without boiling?" Correct response: water slowly evaporates into surrounding air. Avoid 'sunlight destroys water'.
- Conclusion: journal note: "We inferred evaporation from evidence and comparison." No extra XP by default.

## Pedagogy and accuracy gates
The experiment is conceptual and not a numerical simulation; under typical otherwise equal conditions, warmth increases evaporation rate, but airflow/humidity/surface area also affect it. Do not claim dry tabletop proves no leak; an unseen spill remains possible until evidence is considered. Learner must explain what observed evidence supports the conclusion. Optional hint cannot reveal a tested answer when independent evidence is required.
